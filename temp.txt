Challenges for our Group:

    1. Barrel Shifter
    When constructing our barrel shifter, we had several problems with understanding the instructions and with fully grasping the technical requirements of the unit.
    During our initial construction of the unit, we failed to understand that it had to be written using structural VHDL. This required us to rewrite the unit,
    and create the underlying logic for the unit. The barrel shifter was an easy trap to fall into, using the libraries to shortcut
    writing the structural code for the unit also created a failure to understand the technical specifications necessary. Our shifter,
    written with the more advanced libraires did not acutally fulfill the technical requirements, as it would not wrap around when given values over 32.
    This situation could have been avoided, saving us time and effort by more thoroughly reading the project documentation and by understanding the 
    technical requirements of the barrel shifter.

    2. Writing the report for Project 2
    In a similar situation to the barrel shifter, we did not read the project 2 documentation as thoroughly as we should have before
    we started working. While this ultimately did not have as detrimental an effect on our processor's creation and functionality,
    it became a much larger issue when we began writing the report. Once we finished both the SW and HW scheduled pipelines and we began
    working on the reports, it very rapidly became apparent that we should have been working on the report throughout our development period.
    Beginning work on the report after our processor was finished created several pros and cons. In favor of the approach, we were able to complete the
    entire report in a single session, as we had all the data available to us. Against the approach, it slowed us down significantly when we had
    to go back and develop testbenches and lists that would have been beneficial, if not necessary, in our development process.
    This challenge could have been lessened by continuing the report in parallel with our development process, and although it wasn't necessary for
    us as a group, it would have been helpful.

    3. Rewriting the Grendel and Merge sort programs for software scheduling
    A purely technical challenge that was more frustrating and annoying than difficult was rewriting the test programs to be software pipelined.
    While attempting to the best of our abilities to follow the requirements and reduce the number of required NOP instructions, it became readily apparent
    that rewriting the Mergesort program was the best option. While it was frustrating, it was not nearly as challenging as managing the provided
    grendel program. After reviewing the grendel program and failing to modify it multiple times, we came to the conclusion that it was necessary\
    to just use NOPs to avoid hazards. Grendel was challenging to rewrite due to its structure. The labels and program flow were difficult to understand, 
    the code was written without much regard for proper ordering, and the usage of registers was strange to us. Ultimately, the challenge
    was unavoidable and had to be perservered through. 

Hardware Optimization

    1. Single-Cycle
        An improvement to the single-cycle pipeline that we applied to our scheduled-pipelines would be using faster adders like
    lookahead adders or an even more advanced design. Our largest unit in our critical path was by far out Adders, whether that was
    in the ALU or in the PC Fetch Unit. By improving the speed of these adders, we would be able to improve our critical path, 
    provided that the new adder design was faster than ~14ns, the current speed of our adders.

    3. HW-Scheduled Pipeline
        An improvement to our HW-scheduled pipeline would be to implement another register between the ID and EX stages, to break
    up our jumping and branching instructions. Our current critical path is heavily limited by the fact that our PC-updating instructions
    are computed and completed inside of a singular ID stage. This operation, while it has its benefits, caps our maximum cycle time
    and slows down our processor. By computing the PC/REG + IMM operation in the ID stage, and then updating the PC in our EX stage,
    we would be able to split the largest functional unit into a seperate stage, hopefully improving our critical path. The improvement 
    would be unknown, as it would be fully changing the critical path. This would likely change the processor's critical path to be the 
    EX or MEM stage, depending on if DMem or the ALU as a longer cycle time.
